Output

Choose after clicking Begin. Send a copy to BlackHole 2ch to record into a DAW; the rest of your Mac keeps its own output.

Recording into Ableton
  1. Here: also send to → BlackHole 2ch. Keep hear it on on your headphones or speakers.
  2. Ableton → Settings → Audio: Audio Input Device = BlackHole 2ch, Audio Output Device = your headphones or speakers. Match the sample rate to BlackHole's (Audio MIDI Setup shows it).
  3. Same tab → Input Config: turn on the stereo pair 1/2.
  4. An audio track: Audio From = Ext. In, 1/2. Monitor = Off (you already hear Choir directly). Arm it and record.
  5. If takes land a little late, set the track's Track Delay negative, or nudge the clip.
Home: this note brings the tonic (I) or its relative (vi). Rest. Away: a pre-dominant chord (ii, IV). Leaving home. Tension: a dominant (V, vii°, iii) or a chord pulling toward another. It wants to resolve.

A phrase is a short trip: settle on green, step out to red, lean on amber, come home to green. The colors show what each key would bring right now and change after every note. No key is wrong.

The keyboard. The columns are do re mi fa sol la ti do in every key, so the left column is always home. The bottom half of the grid is one octave and the top half the octave above. In minor, the 6th and 7th rise when you climb to them and fall when you come down, as singers do.

Each key is a tension ladder: four chords for the same note, from the most settled at the bottom to the most tense at the top, each lit in the colour of what it does. To resolve, press lower in the key; to build, press higher. After a chord that wants to go somewhere, the pads that take it there pulse: after a dominant (V, vii°), its tonic; after a chord like V/ii, its target (ii); after an away chord, a dominant. The note under the chord tells you what the pulsing means.

Where the phrase goes: the right column. Eight keys: home at the top, then the five keys Bach's chorales most often move to, the parallel major or minor, and one far key. Each is lit by how far it is from where you are: green is a step away (it shares all but one note), amber further, red far. The key you're in pulses. Press one to take the phrase there. The grid re-centres on that key, and two kinds of key light up in turn: amber is the way in (the choir sings the new key's dominant), then green lands on its tonic. Press the key you're already in to end the phrase there the same way: a cadence. Press a blinking button again to stay where you are. (C on the keyboard does the same for the key you're in.)

Top row: the piece's home key down a fifth or up a fifth, major/minor, new piece (back home), latch, then alto, tenor and bass on/off. With latch on (the default), each chord rings until your next note, so you don't have to keep time. N starts a new piece and L toggles latch.
